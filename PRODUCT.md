Many people who deal with recurring headaches, stomachaches, abnormal bowel movements, or skin flare ups tend to seek doctors support, yet leave with treatments that oftentimes don’t cure them, and unanswered questions about the root cause of the symptoms. Furthermore, foods also affect people in ways beyond pain and discomfort, influencing athletic performance, focus, behavior, and overall mood. TasteTrace enables users to reach goals such as heavier weight lifting, staying on task at work, and feeling more energized.

My idea is to create an app, TasteTrace, designed to identify food related symptoms. The user would simply input everything they eat throughout the day and log any symptoms that occur. Then, after a couple weeks, TasteTrace will analyze patterns and determine the foods leading to specific symptoms, which helps the user understand what should be removed from their diet so they can live comfortably. When logging a food for the first time, the app will recommend that the user list out all known ingredients and the preparation of the dish. This helps confirm which specific part causes irritation. For example, if the user simply logs a burger, there is a specific ingredient that they will be sensitive to such as the beef, the bun, or the cooking oil, not the whole dish. If any details are missed, TasteTrace will clarify what exact  ingredients or cooking techniques were used. If the user consumes a specific dish frequently, they can save it as a tile with all the ingredients stored, so the app will immediately recognize what's in it when it is logged again.  
Right now, tastetrace has a functioning website, but I am hoping to turn it into a mobile app soon. I plan to implement new features, notifications, reminders, and tools that make food related lifestyle changes easier. After the app identifies the foods a user should eliminate, it will offer replacements or substitutes that match their eating habits, as well as recommend restaurants in their area that will accommodate their sensitivities. For example, if the app discovers a gluten sensitivity and notices the user regularly eats cookies,  it will recommend gluten free brands and bakeries, or recipes as alternatives. Another feature that will be implemented in the mobile app is branded item recognition. This will ensure that TasteTrace has access to every ingredient in a packaged food, helping it deliver more precise insights. For instance, flaming hot cheetos contain around 20 ingredients. Rather than having to manually recall everything in the snack, the user can simply scan the bar code, or search it with the “find my food” tool. Then, TasteTrace will register the full list of ingredients to be used in later analysis.
