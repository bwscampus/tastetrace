The basics

What is TasteTrace?

TasteTrace is a food and symptom tracker. You log what you eat and how you feel afterward, and TasteTrace looks for patterns to point out the foods most likely linked to your symptoms. Think of it as a detective for your plate: you collect the clues, and TasteTrace helps you connect them.

Why was TasteTrace created?

TasteTrace started with a notebook. Our founder spent months writing down every meal and every reaction by hand before finally figuring out which food was behind her discomfort. TasteTrace is built to make that detective work faster and easier for everyone else.

How is this different from a regular food diary?

A food diary records what you ate. TasteTrace also records how you felt, then does the comparing for you. Instead of flipping back through pages, you get a weekly summary and a ranked list of possible trigger foods.

Who it's for

Who is TasteTrace for?

TasteTrace is for people who regularly have symptoms or just feel off and want to know if food might be part of the reason. It's a good fit if you:

  • Suspect a food is bothering you but can't pin down which one
  • Have tried tracking meals in a notebook or notes app and lost track of the patterns
  • Get symptoms that seem to come and go with no obvious cause
  • Want organized notes to bring to a doctor or dietitian

TasteTrace may not work for everyone. Not every symptom is caused by food, and some patterns take a long time to show up or never show up clearly. If TasteTrace doesn't find a pattern, that doesn't mean nothing is wrong, so keep talking with your doctor about how you feel.

What kinds of symptoms can I track?

Anything you notice. Common examples include bloating, acid reflux, nausea, stomach pain, headaches, skin flare-ups, and fatigue.

If yours isn't on the list, you can write in your own symptom. You can also track things like behavior, focus, and mood if you want to see whether any of them line up with what you ate.

I think I have a food sensitivity. Is TasteTrace for me?

Yes. That's exactly why TasteTrace was built. Logging your meals and reactions over a few weeks can show which foods keep showing up before you feel bad, so you have something concrete to discuss with your doctor.

I already have a diagnosis, like IBS or celiac disease. Is it still useful?

It can be. People who already know about one trigger often want to know if there are others, or want a record of how they feel day to day. TasteTrace works alongside the plan from your doctor or dietitian, not in place of it.

I'm working with a doctor or dietitian. Can TasteTrace help?

Yes. Many care plans ask you to keep a food and symptom diary. TasteTrace keeps that diary for you and can export it as a PDF, so your appointments start with clear, organized information instead of memory.

Is TasteTrace a calorie counter or diet app?

No. TasteTrace doesn't count calories, track weight, or set diet goals. It's about one question: which foods might be connected to how you feel.

Can teens use TasteTrace?

Users aged 13 to 17 can use TasteTrace with a parent's or guardian's permission. TasteTrace isn't intended for children under 13.

Can parents use TasteTrace for their kids?

Yes. A parent or guardian can use their own TasteTrace account to log a child's meals and symptoms, like stomachaches, rashes, or changes in mood, behavior, or focus. It can be hard for kids to explain how they feel, and a simple log helps you spot patterns over time.

You can export the log as a PDF to share with your child's pediatrician. Talk with the pediatrician before removing foods from your child's diet, since kids need a wide range of foods to grow.

Who shouldn't rely on TasteTrace?

If you have a known or suspected serious food allergy, don't use TasteTrace to figure out whether a food is safe to eat. Keep avoiding your known allergens and follow your doctor's plan. TasteTrace is also not a substitute for medical care if your symptoms are severe, getting worse, or come with weight loss, blood in your stool, or trouble swallowing.

Using the app

How do I log a meal?

Pick the meal (breakfast, lunch, dinner, or snack), then add what you ate with a photo or by typing it in. Save your go-to dishes so repeat meals take one tap.

How do I log a symptom?

Open Quick Log, tap what you're feeling, such as bloating, acid reflux, or nausea, and rate it from 1 to 5. You can log it for right now or back-date it if you forgot earlier.

What if I don't know every ingredient, like at a restaurant?

Log what you know. TasteTrace may ask a quick follow-up question, such as the cooking oil or a likely hidden ingredient, to fill in gaps. Meals with unknown ingredients still count, but insights are more reliable when the details are complete.

How long does it take?

Most logs take a few seconds, especially once you've saved your regular meals. The key is consistency: the more meals and reactions you log, the clearer your patterns get.

Can I get reminders to log?

Yes. You can turn on reminders in the app and turn them off anytime, in the app or in your phone's settings.

Insights

How does TasteTrace find my trigger foods?

TasteTrace compares your meal logs with your symptom logs over time. When a food keeps showing up before a symptom, it's ranked as a possible trigger, with a confidence level so you can see how strong the evidence is.

How soon will I see results?

Your Weekly Digest starts after your first week of logging and shows how each day compared with your own baseline. Trigger rankings need more data, so they appear once TasteTrace has seen enough meals and reactions to spot a real pattern. Until then, you'll see a note to keep logging.

What does the confidence level mean?

It shows how much evidence supports each possible trigger. A high-confidence food has shown up before a symptom many times; a low-confidence one is worth watching but might be a coincidence. You can choose how much evidence you want before TasteTrace flags a pattern.

Can I share my results with my doctor?

Yes. From Trigger Insights you can export a PDF evidence ledger of your logs and patterns to bring to an appointment. It can make the conversation with your doctor or dietitian much more specific.

Does TasteTrace use AI?

Yes. TasteTrace uses automated analysis, including AI, to compare your meals and symptoms in the background. There's no chatbot. Insights are possibilities to consider, not conclusions, and they can be wrong, especially when ingredients are unknown.

Privacy & data

Is my data private?

Yes. Your logs are private, and other users can't see your meals, symptoms, or account details. We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Can I delete my data?

Anytime. You can delete individual logs, or delete your whole account from Settings in the app. Account deletion permanently removes your account and logs from our active systems within 14 days.

Is TasteTrace covered by HIPAA?

No. TasteTrace is not a healthcare provider or health plan, so HIPAA doesn't apply. We still treat your meal and symptom logs as sensitive health-related information and protect them accordingly.

Health & safety

Is TasteTrace a medical device or a diagnosis?

No. TasteTrace is a personal tracking tool, not a medical device. It doesn't diagnose, treat, or prevent any condition, and its insights aren't medical advice.

Can TasteTrace tell me if I have a food allergy, celiac disease, or IBS?

No. Only a doctor can diagnose those conditions, often with specific testing. TasteTrace can help you notice patterns and bring organized notes to that conversation.

Should I cut out foods based on my results?

Talk with a doctor or registered dietitian before making big changes to your diet. Cutting out foods without guidance can make some tests less accurate and can leave gaps in your nutrition.

What if my symptoms are severe?

Don't wait on an app. Seek medical care for severe or worsening symptoms. If you have trouble breathing, swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, or other signs of a serious allergic reaction, call 911 or your local emergency number right away.

Good to know

TasteTrace is a personal tracking tool, not medical advice. Always talk with a licensed clinician about health concerns.
